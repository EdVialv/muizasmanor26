# Day 11 — manual coordinate research for the representative sample issues

This is a manual, hand-researched companion to `docs/day11-dry-run-output/day11-dry-run-report.md`'s "Representative sample issues" section (20 of the 3,716 posts, chosen because every one of them was missing `latitude`/`longitude` after row-collapse). It does **not** change the dry-run report itself - that file is generated purely from the workbook and is regenerated, not hand-edited (see its own header comment) - and it does **not** write anything to any database. This is the kind of manual lookup `docs/DAY6_NORMALIZATION_RULES.md` section 5 already calls for ("`needs_review`... keep original text... never auto-correct"), done here with web search/fetch as the research tool instead of personal knowledge of Latvia, exactly as the project owner asked.

Every coordinate below was looked up from a named, cited source - none were estimated, interpolated, or filled in from memory. Where a confident source could not be found, that is recorded plainly rather than guessed.

## What this covers and does not cover

This only resolves `latitude`/`longitude` for 20 sample posts. It does **not** resolve `object_type`, `summary_lv`, `last_verified_at` or `municipality` - those remain open per `docs/DAY11_DRY_RUN_NOTES.md`'s open items, and a resolved coordinate alone does not move any of these 20 posts to `ready_for_import`. Nothing here is staged into `staging_heritage_objects`; this is reference material for whoever does that staging work later.

## Why 12 of 20 could not be resolved

Most of the unresolved posts share a pattern: they are smaller, non-landmark structures (a water mill, a felt factory, a deaf-mute school, a hospital, a specific Orthodox parish in a small town) that either no longer stand, were never individually catalogued by a tourism or heritage site, or share a name common enough (a "watermill," a "water tower") that search cannot distinguish the right building from others with the same generic name. This is not a failure of search effort - each was tried with multiple query phrasings - it reflects that this kind of micro-level building identification genuinely needs the blog post's own text and photos (the actual primary source) cross-referenced against a map, which is exactly the manual-editor step `docs/DAY6_NORMALIZATION_RULES.md` section 5 already anticipates, not something a web search can shortcut.

## Recommended next step

For the 12 unresolved and 2 address-only posts, the most reliable next step is opening each post's own blog URL (`Bloga ieraksta adrese (URL)`, preserved in the raw dataset) and its photos, which may show street signs, nearby landmarks, or text identifying the location precisely enough to place on a map - the same source-verification method `docs/DAY5_COLUMN_MAPPING.md` and `docs/DAY6_NORMALIZATION_RULES.md` already rely on throughout, rather than continuing to search third-party sites that were never built to catalogue this specific dataset's lesser-known structures.
